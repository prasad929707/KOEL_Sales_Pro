## 0. The Honest Truth About Market Share Data

There is no single clean public source for India DG set market share. Anyone who shows you a pie chart with "KOEL: 23%, Cummins: 31%" is either using a paid Frost & Sullivan / Mordor Intelligence report (₹3-4 lakh, not accessible to you) or estimating.

What you CAN do is triangulate from four angles:
1. Listed company financials (Cummins India, Greaves Cotton — both BSE listed, segment disclosed)
2. Volume proxies (revenue ÷ average realization = rough units)
3. KOEL's own disclosures (42,000 units, 39,000 sub-320 kVA — this is your anchor)
4. Channel intelligence signals (visible in earnings call Q&A, analyst reports)

Your output will say "estimated" — and that's fine. The C-suite knows this. What matters is the directional story and the segment-level texture, not the third decimal of market share.

## 3. The Triangulation Method — How to Build Market Share Estimates

### Step 1: Get revenue in Power Gen segment for each player

| Company | FY24 Power Gen Revenue | FY25 Power Gen Revenue | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| KOEL | Find from AR | Find from AR | AR + Investor Deck |
| Cummins India | Find from AR | Find from AR | AR |
| Greaves Cotton | Find from AR (careful with EV mix) | Find from AR | AR |
| Mahindra Powerol | Not available | Not available | N/A |

### Step 2: Estimate average realization per unit

KOEL: ~42,000 units, Power Gen standalone revenue ~₹4,200 Cr (rough) → average realization ≈ ₹10 lakh per unit
This is a blended average across 3 kVA to 12,000 kVA — meaningless on its own but useful as a sanity check.

Better: Segment by kVA range if data allows
- Sub-125 kVA: average ₹3-5 lakh per unit
- 125-750 kVA: average ₹8-15 lakh per unit  
- 750 kVA+: average ₹25-80 lakh per unit
- HHP (2000 kVA+): ₹1-5 Cr per unit

### Step 3: Cross-validate with volume disclosures

KOEL disclosed 42,000 units. If Cummins India's Power Gen revenue is ₹X Cr and we assume similar blended realization, that gives an estimated unit volume. That's your market share proxy.

Don't present this as fact. Present it as: "Based on disclosed revenues and estimated average realizations, KOEL's volume share in the organized market is approximately X-Y%."

### Step 4: Segment-level texture from qualitative sources

This is where earnings call transcripts are gold. Analysts ask:
- Where is growth coming from — LHP or HHP?
- Data center segment — what's the kVA profile?
- How has CPCB IV+ affected product mix?

Management answers reveal segment strategy without giving you exact numbers.

## 4. Key Questions Your Research Should Answer

These are what Mangesh and the C-suite actually care about. Every data point you find should map to one of these.

**Q1: What is KOEL's estimated market share in organized genset market — overall and by kVA band?**
Why it matters: The 2B2B / 4x5y vision requires knowing the starting point. If KOEL is at 18% overall but only 8% in HHP, the growth path is different than if it's 25% overall.

**Q2: Where is Cummins strongest and why?**
Why it matters: Cummins dominates HHP (above 750 kVA) partly because of technology (MTU partnership for large engines) and partly because of relationships with data center developers and large industrial buyers. Understanding this tells KOEL where OptiPrime needs to land.

**Q3: Post-CPCB IV+ — who gained share and who lost?**
Why it matters: The transition was expensive. Smaller players may have lost share. KOEL and Cummins likely consolidated. If true, what's the new competitive landscape?

**Q4: Which segments is each competitor betting on for FY26-30?**
Why it matters: Data centers, green energy, defense — everyone has a different bet. Knowing where competitors are NOT going tells you where KOEL can dominate without fighting.

**Q5: What does Greaves' EV pivot mean for genset competitive dynamics?**
Why it matters: If Greaves is pulling back from gensets to focus on EVs, that's market share up for grabs specifically in LHP.

## 6. What "Market Share" Actually Means Here — Important Nuance

India genset market has two parts:

**Organized market** (KOEL, Cummins, Greaves, Powerol, and a few others): ~60-65% of total units, ~80-85% of total value. These companies manufacture engines and assemble gensets.

**Unorganized / assembled market**: Local assemblers who buy engines from various sources (including Chinese imports) and assemble basic gensets. Dominates sub-62.5 kVA space. CPCB IV+ is supposed to kill this segment — only CPCB IV+ certified gensets can legally be sold. Compliance is imperfect.

Your analysis should focus on organized market share. When you read "KOEL has 20% market share" — it usually means 20% of organized market, not total including unorganized.
